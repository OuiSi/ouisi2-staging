Looking closely

Beta

Goal
Children separate what they observe from what they conclude, then test the conclusion against evidence.
Materials
OuiSi Nature. One card per pair. One device per pair, per table, or just yours — see the note on the right.
Grade 2
Five observations, then I think it is — because I can see —. Steps 1, 2 and close — about 23 min.
Grades 3–4
Ten observations and the reveal — the full 35 min.
Stretch
Two competing explanations of the same card, with the evidence for each.

1 · Only what you can see

8 min

Hand one card to each pair, picture side up. Nobody turns a card over yet.

Say five things you can see. Not what it is — what it looks like.

Shape, color, texture, edges, light. If a child says what it is, take it and ask what they can see that makes them say so.

Now five more.

The second five are the ones worth having. The first five are easy.

2 · Now say what you think

10 min

Same card, same pair. Now they are allowed to guess.

Finish this sentence out loud. I think it is ______, because I can see ______.

The second half is the whole point. A guess without evidence gets handed back: what can you see that makes you say so?

Pairs join into fours and compare.

You have two cards and two guesses. Which guess has more evidence behind it?

3 · Find out

12 min

Now turn the cards over and scan the code on the back. The full photograph opens, along with what it actually shows.

What did the description confirm? What did it contradict? And what did it not settle either way?

The third one is the honest category and children rarely reach for it unaided. A naturalist's description says what the thing is; it often says nothing about the guess a child actually made. Not settled is a real answer here, not a dodge.

Then the question that matters most.

What was easier to notice on the card than in the big photograph?

And what does the big photograph show that the card left out?

Both directions matter, and the second one is usually skipped. The card is a piece of the photograph enlarged, so everything on it is in the original somewhere — it is just competing with everything else. Cropping does not add detail, it removes distraction and makes what is left bigger. Close looking finds things standing back does not, and standing back finds things close looking cannot.

4 · Close

5 min

What can you see now that you could not see at the start?

Watch for

  • Devices. Decide before the lesson, not during. One research station the pairs visit in turn, one device per table, or you scan and project. Any of the three works, and choosing in the moment does not.
  • Naming too early. Children want to say what it is in the first ten seconds. Hold them in step one — the discipline of describing before concluding is the skill being taught.
  • I do not know what it is. Good. Say so. Not knowing is the condition the lesson is built on, and the card is still fully describable.

Adapt

  • Multilingual learners — observations in any language count. The card carries the meaning, and you can supply the English word after.
  • Speech and language — pointing at the part of the card they mean is a full answer. Recast it in words only if it helps.
  • Autistic children — step one asks only for literal observation, with no inference required. Say that it is a complete and correct way to do the step.
  • Want it harder — ask for a second, competing explanation of the same card, with evidence for both.
  • Screen-free settings — skip step three and close after step two, or print the reveals ahead of time.

What's OuiSi?

Pronounced "We-See," OuiSi translates to "Yes Yes" from other languages. It's a nod to our belief that pictures are a language nearly all of us speak. OuiSi is based in the NY Hudson Valley.
