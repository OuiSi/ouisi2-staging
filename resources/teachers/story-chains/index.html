Goal
Children build a sequence that changes from beginning to end, defend the order they chose, and tell it out loud.
Materials
One OuiSi Photo-Card set. Three cards per child.
K–1
Three cards, in an order where something changes. Steps 1, 2 and close — about 20 min.
Grades 2–3
All four steps, and the six-card order becomes a story with a beginning, a change and an ending — the full 30 min.

1 · Three cards each

5 min

Spread the cards face up. Each child takes three.

Take any three. Do not think too hard about it.

Resist helping them choose. Cards picked quickly work as well as cards picked carefully, and the speed keeps the energy up.

2 · Put them in an order

10 min

Put your three cards in an order, so that something changes from the first card to the last.

Something — not someone. It can be a color getting darker, a shape opening up, or a feeling. Leave it wide and see what they do.

Once every child has an order, go around and ask two or three of them one question.

What changed?

If they move a card while answering, that is the lesson working.

3 · Six cards, one story

10 min

Children pair up. Each pair now has six cards and two orders that disagree.

Make one order from all six. You will have to argue about what goes where, and you will have to drop some of what you had.

Then they tell it.

Tell it start to finish, out loud. Take turns — one of you speaks for each card.

If a pair finishes early, give them this.

Pick your middle card. Say one sentence as something inside that picture.

4 · Close

5 min

What was the hardest connection to put into words?

One pair answers, not the whole room.

Watch for

  • Waiting for permission. Young children often want to know whether their order is right. There is no right order, and saying so once, plainly, is usually enough.
  • The silent partner. Taking turns to speak for each card is what prevents one child telling the whole story. Hold to it.
  • Children who like rules can stall without a target. Give them one: every card has to change something from the card before it, with no repeats.

Adapt

  • Pre-readers — nothing here needs reading or writing. It is spoken end to end.
  • Multilingual learners — tell it in any language, then tell one card of it in English. The order carries the meaning either way.
  • Speech and language — pointing along the row while a partner narrates is full participation.
  • Autistic children — a literal sequence, such as small to large, is a correct answer. Say so, and do not ask for a story on top of it.
  • Want it harder — the order has to work read forward and backward, with a different meaning each way.
  • Anxiety — stop at pairs. No telling to the room.

What's OuiSi?

Pronounced "We-See," OuiSi translates to "Yes Yes" from other languages. It's a nod to our belief that pictures are a language nearly all of us speak. OuiSi is based in the NY Hudson Valley.
