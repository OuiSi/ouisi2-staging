How that went

Beta
Grades K–5 Teaching 8–11 min Plus 1 to set out — clearing is the last step Class any size Set any Use after any lesson, once or twice a week
1 min to set out   Take one for how it went 1 min One sentence each 6 min Keep or change 4 min

The whole guide, to read before the lesson.

Goal
A closing routine that gets children to assess the work rather than report a mood, and gets you one usable thing to change next time.
Time
Eight minutes is the floor: all three steps, with the last one held to two answers. The round is what sets the length — one sentence each runs about six minutes with 25 children, the same as the round in Morning meeting, and the cap is the only thing holding it there. Eleven is those same three steps with the keep-or-change opened up, which is what grades 4–5 want. Shorter than eight means dropping the round, not hurrying it — see Watch for.
Materials
One OuiSi Photo-Card set, about fifteen cards face up at the front, or a handful passed along each row.
K–1
Two steps. Drop the keep-or-change — about 7 min
Grades 2–3
All three steps, and the work-not-mood distinction holds — about 8 min
Grades 4–5
Ask what to change rather than what to keep, and let that step run — about 11 min

1 · Take one for how it went

1 min

Cards face up at the front, or passed along the rows. Each child takes one, or points to one if they are not getting up.

Take the card that says how that went. Not how you feel — how the work went.

That distinction is the whole reason this guide exists separately, and children hold it from about grade 2. Below that, how they feel is how it went, and that is fine — do not fight it.

2 · One sentence each

6 min

Round the room, one sentence, then pass it on. Passing is allowed and needs no reason.

My card is ______ and that's how the work went, because ______.

The sentence cap is not a style preference. It is the only thing standing between this and a fifteen-minute discussion at the moment in the day when you have least room for one.

3 · Keep or change

4 min

Looking at the cards on the table — what should we keep next time?

Two answers, then collect the cards. This is the only step that changes your teaching, and it is the one that gets cut when the lesson overruns. Cut the round instead.

Watch for

  • It turning into a mood check. There is already a guide for that. This one asks about the work, and the difference is what makes it worth the end of a lesson.
  • Eating the lesson. If four minutes are left, run steps 1 and 3 with three volunteers and skip the round entirely.
  • Children reading your face. If you only nod at the cheerful cards you will be handed cheerful cards all year.
  • Running it every single lesson. Once or twice a week keeps it worth something. Daily makes it furniture nobody looks at.
  • Acting on one card. One child's hard lesson is not a verdict. Look for the same card showing up three times.

Adapt

  • After any OuiSi activity — this is the closing routine for the rest of the library. Run it after Sort it your way or Draw the connection and the cards are already out on the tables.
  • Multilingual learners — the sentence frame plus a held-up card is enough. One word in the blank is a complete turn.
  • Anxiety — holding the card up without speaking is a turn, and it should get the same nod as a sentence.
  • Grades 4–5 — ask for the change rather than the keep. Harder, less comfortable, and far more useful to you.
  • End of a unit — the same routine over three weeks of work instead of one lesson. The cards stand for the whole thing and the answers get noticeably better.

What's OuiSi?

Pronounced "We-See," OuiSi translates to "Yes Yes" from other languages. It's a nod to our belief that pictures are a language nearly all of us speak. OuiSi is based in the NY Hudson Valley.
