OuiSi · Science

Invent a creature

Beta
Grades 3–5 Teaching 40 min Plus 3 to set out, 3 to clear Class any size Set OuiSi Nature is ideal Prep paper and drawing materials
set out 3′   Hunt for parts 7′ What is it for 10′ The parts decide the place 13′ The parade 7′ Close 3′   clear 3′
Goal
Children build an animal from borrowed parts, justify what each part is for, and then let the parts decide where it has to live — reasoning from structure to habitat.
Materials
One OuiSi Photo-Card set, about ten cards per pair. Paper and drawing materials.
Grade 3
Four parts, each with a job, and one habitat that fits — the full 40 min
Grade 4
Add what it eats, and how the parts make that possible — the full 40 min
Grade 5
Two parts that solve the same problem differently, and which is better where — the full 40 min

1 · Hunt for parts

7 min

Pairs, about ten cards face up. Nothing is drawn yet.

You are building an animal. Find cards that could be parts — a tail, a foot, a hide, a mouth.

It does not matter what the picture actually is. If it could be a tail, it is a tail.

Freeing them from what things really are is what unlocks this. A curl of rope becomes a tail, a cracked wall becomes hide.

2 · What is it for

10 min

Pairs lay their parts out in the shape of the animal.

Arrange your parts. Four or five is plenty.

Then walk round with one question, asked at every pair, about every part.

What is that part for?

This is the science. A pair who cannot answer will usually change the part rather than invent a reason, which is exactly the right instinct and worth naming when it happens.

To anyone who says it looks cool.

That is a reason to draw it. Give me a reason the animal needs it.

3 · The parts decide the place

13 min

Your animal has to live somewhere. Where? And how do you know from its parts?

Put the sentence frame on the board and leave it up.

It has ______, which might help it ______ somewhere ______.

Might, not must — a thick hide suits cold or dry and does not settle which, and wide flat feet suit soft ground without proving it. The logic runs backward from structure to place, and children feel it click. Keep the word might and the reasoning stays honest.

Then the constraint that makes it rigorous.

Now check every other part against that place. Does anything not belong there?

Draw it, name it, and write one sentence about what it eats.

4 · The parade

7 min

Pairs hold up a drawing. The room asks one question each, not a discussion.

Ask them about one part. Why that one?

Could it survive where they said?

5 · Close

3 min

Which part would you change now that you have heard the others?

Watch for

  • Parts with no job. It looks cool is where this lesson goes to die. Every part answers what it is for — gently, but not optionally.
  • Habitat chosen first. Some pairs decide on a desert and then force the parts. Reverse it — the parts are the evidence and the place is the conclusion.
  • Contradictory parts. Thick fur and gills on the same animal. Do not fix it for them. The check at the end of step three is where they find it.
  • Copying a real animal. Ask for one part no real animal has, and the invention starts.

Adapt

  • Grade 3 — four parts and one habitat. Skip the contradiction check if time is short.
  • Grade 5 — require two parts that solve the same problem in different ways, then ask which is better and where.
  • OuiSi Nature — scan the codes on the parts afterward and find what the real organism uses that structure for. Frequently better than anything invented.
  • Multilingual learners — building is wordless, and the sentence frame carries the whole language demand.
  • Autistic children — a precise functional answer is a strong answer. Do not push for whimsy.
  • Tie to a unit — name the habitat up front and have them build an animal that could survive it.

What's OuiSi?

Pronounced "We-See," OuiSi translates to "Yes Yes" from other languages. It's a nod to our belief that pictures are a language nearly all of us speak. OuiSi is based in the NY Hudson Valley.
