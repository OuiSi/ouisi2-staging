OuiSi · Thinking skills

Looking together

Beta
Grades K–5 Teaching 10–25 min Plus 1 to set out, 1 to clear Class tables of 4–6, or a circle Set any Use weekly, all year
1 min to set out   Look, say nothing 1 min Three questions, round after round 22 min What changed 2 min   1 min to clear

The whole guide, to read before the lesson.

Goal
Children make a claim about a picture and back it with something they can point to. The routine never changes, which is what lets it get harder — the cards change, the questions do not.
Time
Ten minutes is the floor, not the length: one card, two or three cycles of the three questions, and the close. Each further cycle costs about three minutes, and a second card doubles the lesson. Twenty-five minutes on one crowded card is a real lesson; so is eight minutes on the way out to recess. Step 2 is the only part that moves.
Materials
One OuiSi Photo-Card set. One card per table, or one card passed round a circle of six. The cards are small, so this does not work as a whole-class image unless it travels.
K–1
One cycle, and a pointed finger counts as an answer — about 8 min
Grades 2–3
Two or three cycles. You paraphrase every answer — about 10 min
Grades 4–5
Four cycles, and they paraphrase each other — about 16 min

1 · Look, and say nothing

1 min

One card in the middle of each table. Everyone looks. You say nothing, and you let the silence run past the point where it is comfortable.

Take a long look. Don't say anything yet.

The silence is doing work. Without it the first child to speak sets the frame and everyone else answers that instead of the card. Time thirty seconds on a clock the first few times — it will feel like a minute.

2 · Three questions, round after round

22 min

What is going on in this picture?

A child answers. Before anyone else speaks, ask that same child the second question.

What do you see that makes you say that?

Then say their answer back and point at the part of the card they pointed at. This is the whole skill of the routine.

So you're saying it's just after a storm, and you're seeing that in the water lying on the ground, here.

Then reopen it and start the cycle again.

What more can we find?

Three or four cycles is the lesson. The questions do not change — not in this lesson and not in April. That is the point: the routine goes invisible and the looking gets harder.

What not to say: good, right, exactly, interesting, or no. The moment one answer earns a word the others did not, children start working out which word you want instead of what they can see.

3 · What changed

2 min

What do you see now that you didn't see in the first minute?

Two or three answers, then stop. It is the only question here that is about them rather than the card, and it is what makes the repetition feel like progress rather than a habit.

Watch for

  • Saying good. The most likely failure and the hardest to stop. It converts the lesson into a guessing game about you.
  • Answering your own question in the paraphrase. So you're saying it's after a storm — because of the puddles, right? has just done the child's work for them.
  • Running it as a quiz. There is no answer on the back of the card, and children find that out fast.
  • Factual errors. Let them stand. Correct them later in the day — a correction inside the conversation ends the looking for everyone in earshot.
  • A card with one object in it. It gives you one cycle and then dies. Choose crowded cards.

Adapt

  • Multilingual learners — pointing is a complete answer to the second question. Take the claim in any language and paraphrase it in English while you point.
  • Anxiety — volunteers only, never a cold call. The silence in step 1 means nobody is behind when the talking starts.
  • Grades 4–5 — hand them the paraphrase. Say back what she just said, then say what you see.
  • Speech and language — the second question is the one that matters, and a finger answers it.
  • Any subject — swap the card for a diagram, a map, or a photograph from the current unit. The three questions do not care what they are pointed at.

Built for repetition. Run this weekly rather than once. The questions are meant to become furniture.
On the questions. This three-question shape comes out of museum education, where it has been used with children for decades and the questions themselves are widely published. The questions are the easy part. The paraphrase is the skill, and it takes a few weeks to get comfortable.
Screen-free. No codes and no devices.

What's OuiSi?

Pronounced "We-See," OuiSi translates to "Yes Yes" from other languages. It's a nod to our belief that pictures are a language nearly all of us speak. OuiSi is based in the NY Hudson Valley.
