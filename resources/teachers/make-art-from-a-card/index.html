OuiSi · Visual arts

Make art from a card

Beta
Grades K–5 Teaching 40 min Plus 3 to set out, 3 to clear Class any size Set any Prep set out art materials
set out 3′   Choose, and say what caught you 6′ Three ways in 6′ Make it 20′ Show, in pairs 8′   clear 3′
Goal
Children choose one card, decide what they are actually responding to in it, and make something that answers it rather than copies it.
Materials
One OuiSi Photo-Card set. One card per child. Paper, and whatever you have — pencils, crayons, paint, pastels, clay.
K–1
Two ways in, not three. Drop the third — the full 40 min
Grades 2–3
All three offered, each child picks one — the full 40 min
Grades 4–5
The third way in is required, and it has to convince from across the room — the full 40 min

1 · Choose, and say what caught you

6 min

Spread the cards face up. Each child takes one they like. They do not have to justify liking it.

Point to the one part of your card that made you pick it up. Just that part, not the whole thing.

Most children reach for a card because of one small thing — a color, an edge, a texture. Naming it now is what keeps the artwork from becoming a copy later.

2 · Three ways in

6 min

Show all three, with your own card, before anyone starts. Each child picks one.

One. Take the part you pointed at and make it the whole picture.

Two. Put the card on your paper and draw what is outside the edges. What is just past the frame?

Three. Say it is something else. This is not a bridge, it is a skyscraper. Then draw the thing you said.

Three choices, not open-ended. Children who freeze when told to make art rarely freeze when asked to pick one of three.

3 · Make it

20 min

They work. Keep the card beside the paper the whole time, not put away.

Circulate, and ask one question — never two.

What did you keep from the card?

The answer tells you whether they are copying or responding. Both are fine at this age. The question is the teaching, not a correction.

To anyone finished early.

Find a second card that would sit next to yours. Do not draw it — just put it there.

4 · Show, in pairs

8 min

Pairs, card and artwork side by side. Not a whole-class show.

Show your partner the card and your picture. Which way in did you choose?

Now your partner answers. What did they keep from the card?

Hearing someone else name what you kept is the strongest moment in this lesson. Leave time for it.

Watch for

  • I can't draw. Common from grade 3 up. The second way in — draw past the edges — has no likeness to fail at, so steer them there before they stall.
  • Careful copying. A faithful copy is a real choice and takes real looking. Name it as such rather than pushing for interpretation.
  • Choosing forever. Give a count of ten for picking a card. The lesson is in what happens after, not in the choice.
  • Children who like rules can stall without a target. Give them one: use only three colors, and they have to come from the card.

Adapt

  • Motor needs — larger paper, chunky materials, and the card taped down so it does not slide.
  • Autistic children — the first way in is the concrete and bounded one. A child who stays there has done the task, and does not need moving on.
  • Multilingual learners — pointing answers every question in this lesson. Words are optional throughout.
  • Anxiety — no sharing at all if needed. The pair step can be silent, cards and pictures placed side by side.
Curriculum links. Identifying what in an image caught your attention · Choosing among compositional strategies — crop, extend, reinterpret · Making work that answers a source rather than copies it · Explaining an artistic choice to a partner.

What's OuiSi?

Pronounced "We-See," OuiSi translates to "Yes Yes" from other languages. It's a nod to our belief that pictures are a language nearly all of us speak. OuiSi is based in the NY Hudson Valley.
